The season

When the snow falls, the Manor glows

Old Town Niagara-on-the-Lake is at its most magical in winter: lights along Queen Street, festive theatre at the Shaw, and the hush of snow over Fort George. Come back to the fireside porch, a glass of wine at our 4 PM social hour, and a quiet room with luxurious linens.

Built in 1832 and renovated in 2024, the Manor has been welcoming travellers in from the cold for nearly two centuries.

Holiday in Old Town

The season, on your doorstep

From festive theatre to icewine tastings, here’s how to spend a winter stay. Your innkeeper is happy to book any of it.

  1. The Shaw Festival’s theatres are a short walk away, and the season carries on into the holidays with festive productions.

    • All three theatres within walking distance
    • Matinees leave the evening free for a long dinner
    • Festive shows tend to sell out, so book ahead

    Innkeeper's tipTell us which show you’re seeing and we’ll book an early dinner nearby, so you’re never rushing to curtain in the cold.

  2. Queen Street dresses up for the season, with lit storefronts, the clock tower and some of Ontario’s best boutique shopping for gifts.

    • Independent shops for one-of-a-kind gifts
    • Cafés and wine bars to warm up in
    • Historic streetscapes that look their best under snow

    Innkeeper's tipGo at dusk, when the lights come on, then stop for a pub supper with live music on the way home.

  3. Icewine grapes are picked frozen on the vine in the coldest weeks of winter. Niagara-on-the-Lake is one of the world’s great icewine regions, and the region celebrates with an Icewine Festival each January.

    • Nearly 40 wineries nearby
    • Château des Charmes, Pillitteri and our favourite, AMO
    • Icewine tastings at many estates

    Innkeeper's tipWe can arrange a winery dinner with the perfect wine pairing. Ask us when you book.

  4. Each winter, Niagara Falls hosts the Winter Festival of Lights, with illuminated displays along the river and the Falls lit in colour after dark.

    • A scenic evening drive along the Niagara Parkway
    • The Falls illuminated after dark
    • Easy to pair with dinner on the way back

    Innkeeper's tipWrap up warm and go after sunset, then come home to the fireside porch for a nightcap.

  5. Fort George, the War of 1812 National Historic Site, sits beside the Manor, and its wooded grounds are beautifully quiet in winter.

    • Forest trails bordering the fort
    • A connection to the Manor’s own 1812 history
    • Lovely in fresh morning snow

    Innkeeper's tipTake a crisp walk before breakfast, and you’ll come back to a warm dining room and three courses.

  6. The lakefront is wild and beautiful in winter, with ice along the shore, big skies, and the gazebo at Queen’s Royal Park.

    • Sunset over the frozen shoreline
    • A short, bracing stroll from the Manor
    • Back in time for the 4 PM wine hour

    Innkeeper's tipBring a thermos. We’re happy to fill it with tea or coffee before you head out.

Event dates change from year to year. Ask us and we’ll check this season’s calendar for your stay.
